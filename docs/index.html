AIM — Artificial Intelligence and Mechanics

Artificial Intelligence and Mechanics for scale-bridging in complex materials: combining mechanics, applied mathematics, and machine learning to understand and predict granular materials across scales.

2025–2029Inria · Université Grenoble AlpesMIAI Cluster / France 2030

About the project

Granular materials—assemblies of interacting grains—are central to industrial processes and environmental phenomena, from powder handling to landslides and avalanches. Their nonlinear, nonsmooth and multiscale behaviour remains difficult to model reliably, especially when predictions must connect individual particles to the behaviour of a large-scale system.

AIM addresses this challenge by bringing together expertise in mechanics, scientific machine learning, numerical simulation and experiments. The research aims to develop physics-grounded data-driven methods for accurate predictions of granular systems at both fine and macroscopic scales.

Research approach

01 / SIMULATION

Particle-scale mechanics

High-fidelity simulations capture contact, friction, impacts and the collective dynamics of granular assemblies.

02 / EXPERIMENTS

In-operando observation

Advanced experimental measurements connect observable behaviour to hidden physical mechanisms.

03 / LEARNING

Physics-constrained AI

Learning methods incorporate statistical physics and non-equilibrium thermodynamics to bridge scales.

Expected impact

AIM seeks proof-of-principle multiscale prediction methods for complex granular materials. Beyond scientific advances, the chair aims to foster open-source software collaboration and interdisciplinary training at the interface of AI and mechanics.

Institutions and support

The AIM chair is funded by the MIAI Cluster and the French National Research Agency (ANR) through the France 2030 programme, under grant ANR-23-IACL-0006, for the period June 2025 to September 2029.
